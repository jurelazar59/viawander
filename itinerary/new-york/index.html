5 Days in New York

Neighbourhood by neighbourhood: the Lower East Side, West Village, Williamsburg, Harlem, and how to eat very well on any budget.

New York is most enjoyable when you stop trying to see it and start trying to live in a part of it for a few hours. Pick a neighbourhood. Get a coffee. Sit somewhere. The city will come to you.

Day 1

Lower East Side & the Manhattan Bridge

Land in the neighbourhood that defined early immigrant New York — Jewish delis, Chinese bakeries, Dominican lunch counters and, now, natural wine bars and art galleries in the same blocks. This tension is the Lower East Side's character and it's still intact.

Morning
Russ & Daughters on East Houston Street has been selling smoked fish, caviar and cream cheese since 1914. Order the Classic (nova lox, cream cheese, capers, red onion on a toasted everything bagel) at the counter. The café around the corner does table service; the original shop does not. The shop is better. Arrive by 9am on weekends.
Lunch
Katz's Delicatessen
Jewish deli · 205 E Houston St · Since 1888 · Cash-friendly
The pastrami on rye with mustard. Not the corned beef, not the matzo ball soup — those are fine. The pastrami is the reason. Hand it to the cutter at the counter and tip him — you'll get a better slice. The ticket system confuses everyone; don't lose yours.
$22–28 per person
Afternoon
Walk across the Manhattan Bridge — the pedestrian path is on the south side, there's a dedicated bike lane, and the view of the Brooklyn Bridge from the middle is better than the view from the Brooklyn Bridge itself. Walk into DUMBO (10 minutes from the bridge landing) and up to Brooklyn Bridge Park. The view of lower Manhattan from Pier 1 at dusk is as good as advertised.
Dinner
Lucali
Pizza · Carroll Gardens, Brooklyn · No reservations · Arrives 4:30pm
The best pizza in New York City by the consensus of people who've eaten all of it. One oval pie per table. No apps. BYOB — the liquor store is one block east. The queue starts forming 90 minutes before opening; show up by 4:30pm and you'll get a table for the first seating.
$28–35 per pie (feeds 2)
Day 2

West Village & the High Line

The West Village is the neighbourhood that most visitors think they want when they say they want "real New York." The brownstones, the small restaurants, the bookshops — it's all still here, just more expensive than it used to be. The High Line connects it to Chelsea and the gallery district.

Morning
Walk the High Line from Gansevoort Street (southern entrance, Meatpacking District) north to 34th Street — it's 2.3km and takes an hour at a comfortable pace. Go before 10am; by noon it's a shoulder-to-shoulder experience. The plantings are specifically designed by Piet Oudolf (the Dutch landscape architect) and the wild-meadow sections between 14th and 20th are particularly good in spring and autumn.
Lunch
Via Carota
Italian · Grove St, West Village · No reservations at lunch · Expect a queue
The best Italian restaurant in the city and it doesn't take dinner reservations (lunch walk-in only, shorter queue). The insalata verde is a salad that will make you reconsider salads. The cacio e pepe is exactly what it should be. The room is small and loud. This is correct.
$45–65 per person
Afternoon
Walk through the West Village streets west of Seventh Avenue — Jane Street, Bank Street, Charles Street — where the brownstones are some of the best-preserved in Manhattan. Three Lives & Company on Cornelia Street is a small, serious bookshop that's been here since 1978. McNally Jackson in SoHo is larger and has a better events calendar. Both are worth an hour.
Subway
Download the MTA app or use Google Maps for transit. Buy an OMNY card at the station (tap-to-pay) or use your phone/contactless card directly — the MetroCard is being phased out. A single ride is $2.90; the unlimited weekly pass ($34) is worth it if you're riding more than 12 times. The subway runs 24/7; the express trains skip local stops and are faster but confusing. Check the route before you board.
Day 3

Williamsburg & Bushwick

Take the L train from Union Square to Bedford Avenue — 20 minutes, two stops into Brooklyn. Williamsburg is worth half a day; Bushwick (three more stops east) is where the murals are and the rent is lower.

Morning
The Smorgasburg outdoor food market runs Saturday mornings at Marsha P. Johnson State Park on the Williamsburg waterfront — 100 food vendors, Manhattan views, reasonably priced. Arrive at 11am, leave by 1pm. The dumplings from Rong Chang and the burgers from Juicy Bao are the reliable anchors. Closed in winter.
Lunch / Early Afternoon
Lilia
Italian · N 7th St, Williamsburg · Reservations recommended
Missy Robbins's restaurant in a converted garage is, by most measures, the best Italian restaurant in the borough. The mafaldini with pink peppercorns and the aglio e olio with sea urchin are the dishes that get discussed. Reserve two weeks out — the walk-in bar at lunch is an option if you can't.
$55–80 per person
Afternoon
Walk east on Metropolitan Avenue into Bushwick — the street art starts around Jefferson Avenue and runs for 20 blocks. The Bushwick Collective murals (technically Jefferson Street between Troutman and St Nicholas) are the most photographed but the side streets have more interesting work. Flushing Ave and Wyckoff Ave have the best concentration. This is a 90-minute walk.
Day 4

Harlem & Museum Mile

Take the 2 or 3 express train to 125th Street. Harlem is the neighbourhood most often talked about and least often visited by tourists, which is a failure of itinerary-writing. The food, the jazz, and the architecture are all worth the ride.

Morning
Marcus Garvey Park (Fifth Avenue and 124th Street) is where Harlem residents actually go on weekend mornings — bring coffee from Harlem Coffee Company on Lenox Avenue and sit in the amphitheatre. The Hamilton Heights neighbourhood west of Amsterdam Avenue has the best examples of late 19th-century row houses in the borough — walk Sugar Hill between 145th and 155th.
Lunch
Sylvia's Restaurant
Soul food · Lenox Ave · Since 1962 · Sunday gospel brunch
The Sunday gospel brunch ($38 buffet, live music at 11am and 1pm) has been a Harlem institution for 60 years. Fried chicken, macaroni and cheese, collard greens, cornbread. Arrive 10 minutes before the first seating. Weekday lunch is quieter and half the price — the fried chicken remains.
Weekday $20–28 · Sunday brunch $38
Afternoon
Walk down Fifth Avenue from 110th Street — this is Museum Mile. The Metropolitan Museum of Art (Fifth Ave at 82nd Street) is the correct choice if you have one afternoon and no museum fatigue: the Egyptian wing (Temple of Dendur), the arms and armour hall, and the rooftop garden in warm months. Give it three hours minimum; the museum is 17 acres and you won't see it all.
Evening
Minton's Playhouse
Jazz club · Cecil Hotel, 118th St · Live music nightly
The room where bebop was invented (Charlie Parker, Thelonious Monk, Dizzy Gillespie played here in the 1940s). Now a dinner-and-jazz club in a beautifully restored space. The music is the point — the food is competent. Reserve for the late show (10:30pm) if you want to stay up.
$30 cover + food/drinks
Day 5

Central Park & Final Morning

Central Park is 843 acres and it functions best when you stop trying to see it as a destination and start treating it as a route between things. Enter from the south, exit from the north, and spend an hour somewhere in the middle.

Morning
Enter at 59th and Sixth Avenue, walk to the Bethesda Fountain (midpark at 72nd Street) — the only planned ornamental fountain in the original Olmsted and Vaux design. Walk north on the Bridle Path to the Reservoir (86th Street) for the Manhattan skyline reflection. Exit at 90th and Fifth, walk to the Neue Galerie (86th and Fifth) for Klimt's portrait of Adele Bloch-Bauer — the museum is small, serious, and opens at 11am.
Final Lunch
Corner Slice, Nolita
Pizza · Spring St · $5 a slice · Standing room
The best regular-New York slice — not the destination pizza, the daily pizza — is at Corner Slice on Spring Street. The square (Sicilian) is the one to order. One slice, one can of something cold, eat standing at the window watching Spring Street. This is the correct final meal.
$5–8
Practical notes
Tipping: 20% at sit-down restaurants, $1–2 per drink at bars, no tip required at counter-service spots. Most restaurants accept cards; carry $40–60 in cash for markets, taxis, and cash-only spots (Katz's, Lucali, a few others). Airport: JFK is 45–60 min by AirTrain + subway ($9.25 total); LaGuardia is 30 minutes by M60 bus ($2.90). Uber from JFK to Manhattan is $50–80 with surge; budget $35–45 off-peak.
