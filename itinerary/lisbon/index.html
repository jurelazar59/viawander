4 Days in Lisbon

Alfama at dawn, pastéis de Belém, Sintra for one afternoon, and where locals actually eat dinner.

Lisbon rewards early risers and late stayers. The city between those hours — when the tour groups retreat and the light goes amber — is one of the best in Europe. Four days is enough to feel it properly.

Day 1

Alfama & Mouraria

Start in Alfama before 9am — the neighbourhood belongs to laundry lines and cats until the miradouros fill up. Walk up from the river via Rua de São Pedro and stop at Portas do Sol for the first view of the estuary.

Morning
Climb to Castelo de São Jorge, but skip the interior — the ramparts are free on weekdays before 10am. Walk back down through Mouraria, the oldest Moorish quarter, to Intendente square for coffee at one of the tile-fronted cafés on the north side.
Lunch
Taberna da Rua das Flores
Portuguese · Chiado · Book ahead
Small plates of things you won't find on menus in the tourist strip — bulhão pato clams, cured meats from Alentejo, a rotating list of natural wines by the glass. Sit at the counter if you're solo.
€28–38 per person
Afternoon
Walk the Rua Nova do Carvalho — Lisbon's most photographed pink street. Duck into LX Factory only if the Sunday market is on; otherwise it's a tourist echo chamber on weekdays. The Museu do Azulejo in the east is worth the tram ride: 25,000 tiles tracing 500 years of the craft, mostly ignored by visitors who stay in the centre.
Dinner
Zé da Mouraria
Tascas · Mouraria · Cash only · No reservations
Eight tables, one daily menu written on a chalkboard, the wine comes in ceramic jugs. The bacalhau à brás has been the same since 1968 and should stay that way.
€15–20 per person
Local tip
Bus 28 (the famous tram) is fine for the view, a nightmare for pickpockets and crowds. Walk the route instead — it's 25 minutes and you control the stops.
Day 2

Belém & the Waterfront

Take the train from Cais do Sodré — 15 minutes to Belém, every 20 minutes, €1.65. Don't take an Uber for this. The train ride past Alcântara and the docks is the point.

Morning
Pastéis de Belém opens at 8am. The queue moves faster than it looks and the egg tarts are genuinely different here — thinner crust, slightly larger, more egg-forward. Eat them at the marble counter with a bica coffee. Then walk 10 minutes to the Jerónimos Monastery — this is Manueline architecture at its peak, best seen at opening time before the river-cruise groups arrive.
Lunch
Tasca do Chico
Fado restaurant · Madragoa
Fado at lunch, which feels indulgent and is. The food (petiscos — small dishes) is secondary to the musicians, who play three rooms away, loud enough to hear clearly. Book the 1pm sitting.
€35–45 per person incl. wine
Afternoon
Walk the Ribeira waterfront from Cais do Sodré to Terreiro do Paço. The Time Out Market is worth exactly one visit — go for the Henrique Sá Pessoa counter in the back corner, skip the rest. Sunset from the Arco da Rua Augusta is free and the crowd disperses quickly after the golden hour photo is taken.
Dinner
Bairro do Avillez
Modern Portuguese · Chiado · José Avillez group
The most reliable mid-range restaurant in Chiado — not a destination meal but genuinely good cooking in a room that isn't trying too hard. The pica pau (pork with garlic) and the bacalhau are the anchors.
€42–55 per person
Day 3

Sintra — the Half-Day Version

Sintra is 40 minutes by train from Rossio station. Go on a weekday if you can — weekends in summer are genuinely unpleasant. Leave at 8am, be back by 3pm, and you'll miss 80% of the crowds.

Morning
Walk up to the Palácio da Pena from the train station — it takes 45 minutes on foot through cork oak forest. The exterior colour scheme (yellow, red, magenta) is either wonderful or appalling depending on your mood. The Palácio Nacional de Sintra in the village centre is more interesting architecturally and usually quieter. Pick one — they're 30 minutes apart and back-to-back feels greedy.
Lunch in Sintra
Tulhas Bar & Restaurante
Traditional · Sintra village · Since 1961
In a converted granary building down an alley off the main square. The açorda (bread soup with egg, coriander, garlic) and grilled fish are what to order. Skip the tourist cafés on the palace approach road.
€18–26 per person
Back in Lisbon — Afternoon & Evening
Return to Lisbon by 3pm and spend the afternoon in Príncipe Real — the neighbourhood west of Chiado with the better bookshops, independent clothing designers, and a garden square worth sitting in. Dinner here at Taberna Fina or Solar dos Presuntos (the latter for a more formal, old-Lisbon experience worth having once).
Day 4

Chiado, Bica & Departure

The last morning is for the things you keep putting off. Walk the Bica funicular for the view — it costs €3.90 return and the view from the top of the Calçada do Combro is worth it. Then coffee and slow reading in A Brasileira on Rua Garrett.

Morning
A Brasileira opened in 1905 and the interior has barely changed. The coffee is fine — the point is sitting where Fernando Pessoa wrote and watching the street. Then walk to Livraria Bertrand on Rua Garrett: the oldest operating bookshop in the world (1732), and the English-language section has good titles. Spend an hour.
Final Lunch
The Mill
Coffee & light food · Príncipe Real
The best specialty coffee shop in the city and the avocado toast is forgiven because everything else is done properly. The flat white uses beans from Minas Gerais and tastes like it should.
€12–18 per person
Where to stay
The Internacional Design Hotel on Rossio square is a design hotel that earns the label — rooms are themed around global artistic movements and the location puts you 8 minutes from everything. Mid-range. For budget, Lisbon Story Guesthouse on Largo São Cristóvão in Alfama is family-run and among the most honest accommodations in the city.
