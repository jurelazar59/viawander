5 Days in Tokyo

A neighbourhood-first itinerary: old Yanaka, jazz bars in Shimokitazawa, ramen at 11pm, and one morning in Kamakura.

Tokyo rewards the visitor who stays still more than the one who moves fast. Each neighbourhood has its own complete logic — spend a morning in one and you begin to understand it. Try to see six districts in a day and you've seen none of them.

Day 1

Yanaka & Ueno

Yanaka is the part of Tokyo that survived the firebombing of 1945 and the subsequent reconstruction. The streets are narrow, the buildings are mostly pre-war wood, and the population is older. It's a reasonable approximation of what the whole city once looked like.

Morning
Walk Yanaka Ginza — the shotengai (covered shopping street) opens at 9am and the small shops selling sembei, dried fish, tofu, and traditional crafts are better understood by walking slowly than photographing quickly. The cemetery at Yanaka Reien is not morbid but genuinely peaceful — Tokugawa Yoshinobu, the last shogun, is buried here. Walk it before the neighbourhood wakes up.
Lunch
Kayaba Coffee
Kissaten · Yanaka · Opens 8am
A Showa-era coffee shop that's been in this building since 1938 (restored after a fire, reopened 2009). The morning set — thick toast, egg, salad, coffee — is the correct meal here. Order before 11am.
¥850–1,200 per person
Afternoon
Walk south 15 minutes to the Tokyo National Museum in Ueno — the largest art museum in Japan, and the permanent collection of Japanese art (Honkan building) is world-class. The room of samurai armour alone is worth the ¥1,000 admission. The park around Shinobazu Pond is worth 20 minutes of walking between buildings.
Dinner
Ippudo Ramen, Nakameguro
Ramen · Hakata style · Queue expected
The shiromaru moto (white pork broth, thin noodles, cabbage) at around 7pm when the queue is manageable. Sit at the counter. Order a kaedama (extra noodles for ¥110) when the bowl is half empty.
¥900–1,400 per person
Day 2

Shimokitazawa & Setagaya

Shimokitazawa is where Tokyo's musicians, writers and vintage obsessives live. It's a 15-minute train from Shinjuku and feels like a different city — narrow streets, no large stores, 200 live music venues in a 1km radius.

Morning
The vintage clothing shops (Flamingo, Yuke's, and the cluster around Honda-za theatre) open at 11am. Spend the morning in the covered market under the former train tracks — the Shimokitazawa Bonus Track development has food stalls, a brewery and record shop in converted rail arches. Arrive hungry.
Lunch
Bear Pond Espresso
Specialty coffee · Shimokitazawa · Cash only · Opens noon
The espresso here is singular — dense, sweet, and brewed by the owner, who has been pulling shots here since 2009 and has the whole thing down to a discipline. The single-origin pour-over changes monthly. Don't arrive before noon; the owner won't serve you.
¥600–800
Evening
Shimokitazawa live music: DUO Music Exchange for bigger acts, Garage for jazz, Mother for indie. Shows start at 7 or 8pm; cover is ¥2,000–4,000 including one drink. The line-ups are posted outside each venue. Walk in based on what you hear through the door — this works better than planning.
IC Card
Buy a Suica or Pasmo IC card at any JR station on arrival day. Load ¥3,000. It covers metro, buses, JR trains and most convenience store purchases. You'll spend nearly nothing on transport in central Tokyo — most journeys are ¥200–300.
Day 3

Tsukiji, Hamarikyu & Ginza

The wholesale fish market moved to Toyosu in 2018. The outer market at Tsukiji — the part with the food stalls, knife shops, and tamagoyaki vendors — is still there and still worth 7am.

Early Morning
Tsukiji outer market opens before 7am. Walk the main alley (Uogashi Yokocho) and eat at the counter of any stall serving tamago (egg) sushi or grilled oysters. The knife shops (Tsukiji Masamoto, Aritsugu) are worth 20 minutes each — the carbon-steel knives start at ¥15,000 and will last 30 years.
Lunch
Sushi Saito (if you have a connection) / Sushi Yoshitake
Omakase · Requires introduction or very early reservation
Sushi Saito is the best sushi restaurant in the world by most accounts and requires a Japanese-speaking referral to book. Sushi Yoshitake (3 Michelin stars, Ginza) takes reservations from overseas and the 11:30am lunch omakase is ¥33,000 — substantial but worth it once in a life.
Lunch omakase from ¥18,000
Afternoon
Walk through Hamarikyu Gardens (¥300) — a 17th-century shogunate garden on the edge of Tokyo Bay, surrounded by skyscrapers, which makes the contrast work. Then into Ginza for the department store basement floors (depachika): Isetan in Shinjuku is better for food, but the Matsuya basement on Chuo-dori has excellent wagashi (traditional sweets) counters and a good selection of regional snacks from across Japan.
Day 4

Kamakura Day Trip

Take the JR Yokosuka Line from Tokyo station to Kamakura — 53 minutes, ¥940 each way. Leave at 7:30am to beat the crowds at the Great Buddha by 90 minutes. The entire Kamakura circuit is best done in half a day; the seaside town itself is pleasant for an afternoon.

Morning
Walk the Daibutsu Hiking Course (2.5km, 70 minutes) from Kitakamakura station to the Great Buddha — it passes through cedar forest and connects three zen temples before descending to Kotoku-in. The Buddha itself (13.35m, cast in 1252) is larger in person than photographs suggest. You can go inside for ¥20.
Lunch
Bowls Kamakura
Café & light food · Near Yuigahama beach
Acai bowls, whole grain rice bowls, good coffee — the kind of place that doesn't look like it should work in a 13th-century temple town and works very well. Walk to Yuigahama beach (10 minutes from Hase station) and eat on the sand if the weather is right.
¥1,200–1,800 per person
Afternoon & Return
Walk the Komachi-dori shopping street back toward Kamakura station — the shops selling pickles, sembei and Buddhist craft goods are genuinely local. Return to Tokyo by 4pm and spend the evening at Shibuya crossing (the famous pedestrian scramble, best at dusk from the Shibuya Sky observation deck or the free viewing spot at the Starbucks on the corner).
Day 5

Shinjuku & Final Evening

Shinjuku is the loudest, most vertical, most overwhelming part of Tokyo. It works best on a last night when you're ready to absorb it rather than navigate it.

Morning
The Tokyo Metropolitan Government Building observation deck is free and opens at 9am — the view to Fuji (clear days, morning only) is the best free viewpoint in the city. Walk through Shinjuku Gyoen National Garden (¥500) before it gets busy — 58 hectares of formal Japanese garden in the middle of the ward, and the greenhouse has tropical plants worth seeing.
Lunch & Afternoon
Isetan Shinjuku — B2 Food Hall
Department store · 3-24-1 Shinjuku
The basement food floors of Isetan are a serious education in Japanese food culture — the bento counter, the wagyu butcher, the sushi counter, the crêperie, the champagne bar. Buy a bento (¥1,500–3,000) and eat in Shinjuku Gyoen. This is the correct Tokyo lunch.
¥1,500–3,000 per person
Final Dinner
Omoide Yokocho (Memory Lane)
Yakitori alley · West exit Shinjuku station · From 5pm
Thirty tiny yakitori stalls under the train tracks, each with 8–10 seats, all of them smoking. Order tare (sauce) chicken skewers, drink Sapporo from a can, sit next to a salary man who's been coming here since 1983. This is not the best food in Tokyo; it might be the most Tokyo.
¥2,500–4,000 per person incl. drinks
Getting around
Google Maps works perfectly for Tokyo transit in English — use the "transit" option and it'll give you the exact platform, car number, and transfer instructions. The metro and JR lines overlap but aren't the same network. Your Suica card covers both. Don't take taxis unless it's raining after midnight.
