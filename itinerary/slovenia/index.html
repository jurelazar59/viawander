4 Days in Slovenia

Ljubljana's old town, Lake Bled before the kayaks arrive, the emerald Soča, and an Adriatic evening in Piran.

Slovenia is the size of New Jersey and has a Tripadvisor problem — it became known too quickly. But it's still possible to have Bled to yourself if you know when to show up, and the Soča Valley remains one of the least-photographed green rivers in Europe.

Day 1

Ljubljana

Ljubljana is a 45-minute drive from any airport and about 90 minutes by bus from Venice or Zagreb. It's a proper European capital that behaves like a small city — the old town is waleable in an afternoon, but there's enough neighbourhood texture to fill two days.

Morning
Walk up to Ljubljana Castle before 10am — the funicular is worth skipping, the path up through the old town takes 12 minutes and the view from the battlements at dawn is reliably good. The history museum inside is decent for an hour. Come down through Stari Trg (Old Square) and walk to Vodnikov trg market for coffee at one of the standing-room spots by the flower stalls.
Lunch
Gostilna na Gradu
Slovenian · Ljubljana Castle · Seasonal menu
Inside the castle walls, which sounds like a tourist trap and isn't. The kranjska klobasa (smoked sausage) with mustard and sauerkraut is the benchmark dish — order it here first to understand what you're eating for the rest of the trip.
€22–32 per person
Afternoon
Cross Tromostovje (Triple Bridge) and walk along the Ljubljanica river. The riverside café strip is for sitting, not photographing — get a pivo (beer) at Nebotičnik on Štefanova for the 10th-floor terrace view. The National Gallery on Cankarjeva is free on the first Sunday of the month; the permanent collection is worth an hour if it's not.
Dinner
Restavracija Monstera Bistro
Modern Slovenian · Wolfova ulica · Book ahead
The most interesting restaurant in the city and genuinely affordable for the quality. The menu changes with what's available at the morning market. Sit at the kitchen counter if you can.
€35–48 per person
Day 2

Lake Bled

Drive or take the bus to Bled (1.5 hours). This matters: be there by 7am. The lake is genuinely extraordinary before the first pletna boats launch. By 10am it's a selfie queue; by noon it's a theme park. Plan accordingly.

Morning
Walk the lake circuit (6km, 90 minutes at a comfortable pace) before breakfast. The west shore viewpoint above Ojstrica gives the postcard angle — it's a 20-minute steep climb from the parking area but early morning has it empty. Then breakfast at Penzion Mayer on the north shore, which has been making kremšnita (cream cake) since 1952. The version at the Park Hotel café is the famous one but Mayer's is better and quieter.
Midday
Take the pletna boat to Bled Island — it costs €18 return and includes the 99-step climb to the church. Ring the bell, which is a wishing-well ritual, and look at the frescoes before heading back. The island chapel is genuinely beautiful and takes 20 minutes. Don't bring lunch — there's nowhere decent to eat on the island.
Afternoon
Drive up to Vintgar Gorge (8km north of Bled) for the afternoon, when the light hits the water at the right angle. The 1.6km wooden boardwalk above the Radovna river is one of the better walks in the Julian Alps and takes about an hour each way. Opens at 8am, closes in autumn.
Sleep in Bled
Penzion Mayer (mentioned above) is the best mid-range option on the north shore — family-run, breakfast is the meal of the trip, lake views from rooms 8–12. Book the west-facing rooms specifically.
Day 3

Soča Valley

Drive west from Bled through the Vršič Pass (the highest mountain road in Slovenia at 1,611m) to the Soča Valley. Allow 2.5 hours for the drive — the 50 hairpin bends are the point, not an inconvenience. The Soča river runs from pale turquoise to emerald green depending on depth and time of day; photos don't do this accurately.

Morning
Stop at the Russian Chapel on the Vršič Pass (built by Russian POWs in 1916) and at the Mojstrovka viewpoint at the summit. Then descend to the Soča river at Trenta — the source spring is a 20-minute walk from the car park and the water temperature is 5°C year-round.
Lunch
Hiša Franko
Fine dining · Kobarid · Ana Roš · Book months ahead
Ana Roš's restaurant in a farmhouse outside Kobarid is among the best in Europe — ingredients from the valley within 5km, a tasting menu that takes 3 hours. Book four months ahead. If it's full, the lunch tasting menu at Topli Val in Kobarid is a very good second.
€180–220 tasting menu · Lunch from €85
Afternoon
Kobarid has one of the best WWI museums in Europe — the Kobarid Museum covers the Isonzo Front (eleven battles here between 1915–1917) with detailed maps and personal accounts. Caporetto is the Italian name for this town. Hemingway set the retreat scene of A Farewell to Arms on this road. Give it two hours.
Day 4

Piran & the Adriatic

Slovenia has 46km of Adriatic coastline. Piran, at the southern tip, is the piece worth seeing — a Venetian-era walled town on a narrow peninsula. Drive from the Soča Valley in about 90 minutes via Tolmin and Nova Gorica.

Morning
Park outside the walls (cars are restricted inside) and walk in through the Porta Dolfin gate. Climb the campanile of St. George's Cathedral before 10am — it's 15m tall and the view across the old town rooftops to the sea is the reason to come. The walk around the Punta headland (the tip of the peninsula) takes 20 minutes and ends at the harbour front.
Lunch
Pri Mari
Seafood · Piran · Family-run · Terrace on the water
The grilled brancin (sea bass) with olive oil and lemon, and the octopus salad. Order whatever came in that morning — ask them. Wine from the Karst region (Teran, a dark red from iron-rich soil) is the local pairing.
€28–42 per person
Afternoon
The salt pans at Sečovlje (10 minutes south of Piran) have been working since the 14th century and still produce hand-harvested fleur de sel. The visitor path through the pans takes 45 minutes and the museum in the restored salter's house explains the process clearly. Buy salt at the shop at the entrance — it travels well and is a better souvenir than anything in Piran's tourist market.
Practical notes
A rental car is not optional for this itinerary — public transport connects Ljubljana and Bled but the Soča Valley and Piran require a car. Budget €35–55/day from Ljubljana airport. Petrol station in Kobarid before driving the Vršič — it's 80km of mountain road before the next one.
